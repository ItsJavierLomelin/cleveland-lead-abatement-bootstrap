Lead Abatement in Collinwood, Cleveland

Collinwood was a separate village until 1910, built up around the New York Central rail yards and the factories that surrounded them. Homes built for that workforce are old enough that lead paint is a fair assumption until a test says otherwise. North and South Collinwood both have arts-district revival along Waterloo Road alongside older houses that need attention.

Rail yards, factories and a long decline

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History describes Collinwood as one of the most concentrated industrial areas in the world during World War II, with Fisher Body, General Electric and Lincoln Electric among the employers. North Collinwood's population fell from 27,590 in 1940 to about 16,700 and South Collinwood's from 28,438 to about 10,500.

Fewer residents on the same street grid means more empty houses and more deferred maintenance, and deferred maintenance is how paint deteriorates.
