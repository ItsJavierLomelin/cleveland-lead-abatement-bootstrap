Lead Paint Testing and Removal in Detroit-Shoreway, Cleveland

Streetcars reached Detroit Avenue in 1863, and dense housing grew around it as lakefront factories pulled workers in, so by 1920 the neighborhood held about 41,000 people. A lot of that housing still stands around Gordon Square, with rentals and rehabs on the same blocks. That mix is where inspections, rental certificates and careful renovation all collide.

Dense housing from the streetcar era

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History describes Yankee, German, Irish, Italian and Romanian settlement across the area and notes that Detroit-Shoreway Community Development Organization has helped preserve more than a dozen historic buildings and create hundreds of housing units. Population fell from 41,000 in 1920 to 17,382 in 2000, and then growth returned around Gordon Square.

Houses packed close together on small lots are a specific lead scenario: dust and paint chips from one property can reach the next, and exterior work on one house affects neighbors.

Common questions

I rent out a double near Gordon Square. Do I need a certificate?

If it was built before 1978, Cleveland requires a lead safe certificate or an exemption for the rental. The first step is a lead risk assessment.

Does exterior work on a close neighbor affect my yard?

Exterior scraping and sanding can drop paint into neighboring soil. Ask contractors on either side about lead-safe practices, and consider soil testing if children play outside.
