Lead Abatement in Glenville, Cleveland

Glenville was incorporated as a village in 1870 and annexed to Cleveland in 1905, and its lakeshore setting drew racetrack crowds and summer estates in the late 1800s. Older homes from those decades remain part of the housing mix near Gordon Park and St. Clair Avenue. Houses of that age call for lead testing before repairs, sales or rentals.

Lakeshore village to city neighborhood

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History recounts that the Glenville Race Track opened in 1870 at St. Clair Avenue and East 88th Street, that William J. Gordon's 122-acre lakefront estate became Gordon Park, and that the neighborhood absorbed the former Forest Hills area in the mid 2010s.

Neighborhoods that were built over many decades have a mix of construction years, which is why checking the year built is the first step for any house.

What families and landlords ask here

Parents ask about the home environment after a child's screening, and the guide to a high blood lead result explains where to start. Owners of rentals ask about the city's certificate, covered on the page about lead safe certificates. Common questions

Does Glenville include the former Forest Hills area?

According to the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, Glenville absorbed the former Forest Hills neighborhood in the mid 2010s, so its boundaries extend east to about East 125th Street.

Who should I ask about a child's lead test?

Start with the child's health care provider and the local health department. For the house, a risk assessment checks paint, dust and soil.
