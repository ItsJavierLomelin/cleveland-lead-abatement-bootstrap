Lead Paint Abatement in Tremont, Cleveland

Tremont holds some of the oldest housing in Cleveland: the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History reports that by the 1980s, 68 percent of its housing had been built before 1900. Hundreds of homes have since been renovated, and each renovation of an old painted surface is a chance to stir up lead dust. The steep streets around Lincoln Park are full of houses that need a lead check before work starts.

A neighborhood of very old houses

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History traces Tremont's industrial base to the Lamson and Sessions Company in 1869, with Irish and German workers in the 1860s, then Poles, Greeks, Syrians, Ukrainians and Puerto Ricans in later decades. Houses built for those workers have had many owners and many coats of paint.

The same source notes that hundreds of Tremont homes have been renovated, while many long-time owners and older renters still live with aging paint and aging windows.

What that means for lead

A house built before 1900 predates the 1978 ban by almost a century, so the paint history is long. Tenants and owners in Tremont often face deteriorating window sashes, porch parts and exterior trim. A lead risk assessment sorts out which of those are actual hazards, and lead-safe window replacement is a common fix for the windows that grind paint into dust.

Common questions

My Tremont house is over 100 years old. Does that make lead more likely?

Older paint history means more layers and more chances for lead, and EPA reports that 87 percent of homes built before 1940 have some lead-based paint. Testing confirms what is on your surfaces.

I bought a fixer-upper near Lincoln Park. Can I scrape the porch myself?

EPA advises against removing lead-based paint yourself. Test the paint first and use trained, certified people for work that disturbs it.
