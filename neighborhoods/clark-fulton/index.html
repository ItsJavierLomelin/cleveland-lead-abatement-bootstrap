Lead Paint Testing and Abatement in Clark-Fulton, Cleveland

Clark-Fulton packs about 11,000 people into roughly one square mile, making it the most densely populated neighborhood in Cleveland, and the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History notes that one- and two-family homes were already abundant here by the late 19th century. Houses on tight lots stand close together, so a project that sheds paint chips or dust on one property reaches the next one quickly.

One- and two-family homes close together

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History lists Germans, Slovaks, Czechs, Poles and Italians among the early residents, many working in breweries along Train Avenue and in the Flats, and describes Puerto Rican families arriving from the 1960s. St. Rocco's Parish and the old Aragon Ballroom building, built in 1905 as a skating rink, are among the landmarks.

In a neighborhood with this many older one- and two-family houses, many are rented, and rentals built before 1978 fall under the city's certificate rule.

Practical lead steps for dense blocks

Close neighbors mean dust control matters more. When exterior paint is scraped or windows are replaced, containment keeps debris on the property. Lead-safe window replacement and abatement both rely on that. Afterward, clearance testing confirms the dust inside is down.

If you are a tenant worried about chipping paint, the city's lead page explains the program, and how to tell if paint is lead paint explains what to look for.

Common questions

I rent a two-family in Clark-Fulton. Who handles lead problems?

Cleveland puts the certificate burden on rental owners of pre-1978 properties. Tenants can ask whether the unit has a certificate.

Does lead dust travel between close houses?

Exterior scraping, sanding and demolition can drop paint onto neighboring yards, which is why lead-safe work uses containment.
