Lead Abatement in Ohio City, Cleveland

Ohio City was its own city from 1836 until Cleveland annexed it in 1854, and most of its older buildings date to the middle and late 19th century. Frame and brick houses around the West Side Market have been repainted and restored many times, which is how old lead coatings end up under newer layers. Find out what is under the paint before anyone sands it.

What the housing here looks like

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History describes a neighborhood that declined after World War II and then saw more than 100 structures restored or redeveloped between 1963 and 1978, with residential revitalization still strong today. A century and a half of paint layers plus decades of restoration work is the situation where a lead inspection before renovation matters most.

Restoring a historic house usually means touching original trim, porches and windows, exactly the surfaces that tend to carry the oldest paint.

Where lead shows up in houses like these

Original wood windows are the first suspects, followed by porch posts, railings and the exterior trim that gets repainted every few years. On a block of older homes close together, flaking exterior paint also lands in yards and shared walkways, so soil near the foundation is worth a look. Dust and soil testing covers both.

Common questions

I am restoring an old house near West 25th Street. Where do I start?

Start with testing so you know which surfaces carry lead, then plan the restoration around them. If a contractor will disturb the paint, ask for EPA lead-safe renovation certification.

Do Ohio City rentals need a lead safe certificate?

If the property is a rental built before 1978, Cleveland requires a certificate or an exemption. See lead safe certificates for Cleveland rentals.
