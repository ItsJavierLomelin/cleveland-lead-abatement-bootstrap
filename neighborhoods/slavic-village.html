Lead Abatement in Slavic Village, Cleveland

Slavic Village grew around the Cleveland Rolling Mills, with Czech families in Karlin and Polish families around St. Stanislaus Church from the 1880s. That worker housing is now well over a century old, and many homes sat neglected during the foreclosure crisis that hit ZIP code 44105 harder than any other area in the country. Peeling exterior paint and tired porches are where lead trouble usually starts.

Worker housing and a hard recent history

According to the entry for South Broadway and the entry for North Broadway in the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, the two neighborhoods together are known as Slavic Village, and Poles settled the Warszawa section near Fleet Avenue to work at the rolling mills. ZIP code 44105 recorded more home foreclosures than any other area in the country during the Great Recession.

Vacant and underinvested houses are where paint deteriorates unnoticed. Revitalization work through Slavic Village Development has put new housing into the area, and every rehab of an old house in the neighborhood is a lead question.

Where lead checks matter most

Buyers rehabbing a long-vacant house should test before demolition, because years of weathering can leave exterior paint flaking into the soil and interior paint chalking on sills. After a rehab, clearance testing and cleanup confirms that the dust from the work is gone before a family moves in.

Landlords with pre-1978 rentals in Slavic Village are covered by the city's certificate rule, which is explained on the page about lead safe certificates.
