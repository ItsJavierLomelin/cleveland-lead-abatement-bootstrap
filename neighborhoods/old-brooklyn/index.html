Lead Paint Abatement in Old Brooklyn, Cleveland

Old Brooklyn began as the hamlet of Brighton in 1814, became the village of South Brooklyn in 1889 and joined Cleveland in 1905. Market gardens once filled the area, which is worth knowing because garden beds and yard soil near an old painted house are places lead can settle. Today it is a residential neighborhood of about 33,000 people near Brookside Park.

A village that became a neighborhood

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History records that South Brooklyn was a market-gardening center, that public transportation reached the community's borders in 1869, and that Cleveland took in the village in December 1905 and added neighboring pieces of Brooklyn Township by 1917. The Old Brooklyn Community Development Corporation formed in 1976 and has seen refurbished homes and new businesses in recent years.

A neighborhood that grew across several decades has houses from several eras, so one block can mix homes that fall before and after the 1978 line.

Yards, gardens and older houses

Gardening is where soil testing comes up. If your house predates 1978 and the exterior has been scraped or has weathered, soil sampling along the foundation tells you whether to build raised beds or move the garden. Inside the house the usual suspects are windows and stairs.

If you are remodeling a house in the area, test first and use certified renovators. See testing, abatement or renovation.

Common questions

Is my Old Brooklyn house covered by the lead rules?

Rules depend on the year the house was built. Look up the year on the county property record, and if it is 1978 or earlier, treat lead paint as possible until tested.

Can I plant vegetables next to my foundation?

Ask for soil sampling first if the house is older than 1978 and the exterior paint has flaked.
