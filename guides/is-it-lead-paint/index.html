How to Tell If Your Paint Is Lead Paint

People search for this when they notice paint flaking off an old window or porch rail and wonder whether it is dangerous. The honest answer is that you cannot tell by looking, but the age of the house and the condition of the paint tell you how seriously to take it.

Start with the age of the house

Lead was banned in residential paint in 1978. EPA says that 87 percent of homes built before 1940 have some lead-based paint, and 24 percent of homes built between 1960 and 1978 do. The City of Cleveland graphic on its lead page notes that about 80 percent of homes in the city were built before 1980, so for a house in Cleveland the age question usually points toward testing. You can find the year built on the county property record.

Signs that deserve attention

Look at paint that is peeling, chipping, chalking or cracking. Look hard at the parts of the house that rub or take impact: window sashes and sills, door frames, stair treads and railings, and porch floors and posts. The EPA family booklet names these same areas. Fine, powdery residue on a window sill is also worth taking seriously, because that is how lead dust shows up in practice.

Lead paint is not always visible damage. Intact lead paint under newer layers can be fine until someone sands, scrapes or heats it during a remodel.

What not to do

Do not dry scrape, sand or burn the paint to find out what is under it. That is the fastest way to turn a stable surface into a dust problem. EPA's advice in the booklet is to keep painted surfaces in good condition, get your home checked by a certified risk assessor, and not to try removing lead-based paint yourself.

How you find out for sure

Confirming lead paint takes testing. A lead paint inspection says which surfaces contain lead, and a risk assessment says which of those are hazards. If you are comparing options, the guide on testing, abatement or renovation helps you pick.

On symptoms: CDC notes that a child with lead poisoning may not have visible signs or symptoms, so a blood test is how exposure gets found. See what to do after a high blood lead result.

Common questions

Can I use a home test kit?

A kit only checks the spot you swab. A full inspection or assessment covers the whole home and is done by licensed people, which matters if you plan to rent, sell or repair based on the result.

My house was built in 1985. Do I need to worry?

The ban on residential lead paint took effect in 1978, and the City of Cleveland says houses built after 1978 are exempt from its lead safe certificate law. Older layers or old components added later are the exceptions to ask about.
