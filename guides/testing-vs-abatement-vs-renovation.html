Lead Testing, Abatement or Renovation: Which Service Do You Need?

Callers rarely start with the right service name. They say there is paint peeling on a porch, a landlord notice, a kid who tested high, or a kitchen they want to gut. This guide sorts those situations into the service that actually fits.

You are not sure there is lead at all

If the house was built in 1978 or earlier and you do not know what is in the paint, start with testing and inspection. It tells you which surfaces contain lead without committing you to any work.

Someone may already be getting exposed

A child with a high blood test, a pregnant household member, or visible dust and chips around a window point toward a risk assessment, which looks at hazards, and often dust and soil testing. The page on a high blood lead result covers the first steps at home.

You need to fix a known hazard

When an assessment or an order calls for lead hazard control, that is abatement: removal, replacement, or encapsulation and enclosure. EPA separates abatement from renovation work by purpose: abatement exists to address lead, while renovation disturbs paint as a side effect.

You are remodeling and lead is a side issue

A kitchen, bath or porch project in a pre-1978 home is renovation. Paid renovation work in those homes falls under EPA's renovation, repair and painting rule, which requires certified firms and lead-safe work practices. See renovating a pre-1978 Cleveland house.

You own a Cleveland rental

The city requires a certificate or exemption for pre-1978 rentals, and the process begins with an assessment. The page on lead safe certificates has the steps.

Common questions

What is the difference between abatement and renovation?

Abatement is work done to permanently eliminate lead paint hazards. Renovation is any other work that happens to disturb paint, and it still needs lead-safe practices in older homes.

Do I need clearance testing after every job?

Clearance examinations follow abatement. Renovation has its own cleaning verification steps. See clearance testing and cleanup.
