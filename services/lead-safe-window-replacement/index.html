Lead-Safe Window and Door Replacement for Old Cleveland Houses

Old wood windows are the classic source of lead dust in a Cleveland home: every time a painted sash rubs a painted frame, a little paint becomes powder on the sill. Replacing the sash, or the whole window, removes the lead paint on that part when the work is done with lead-safe methods.

Why windows and doors come first

EPA lists windows and window sills, doors and door frames, stairs and porches among the most common places for lead hazards, because they take friction and impact. A painted window that sticks, rattles or gets opened daily is a steady dust source, and the dust gathers right where children play near sills and floors.

That is why many abatement plans treat windows separately from walls. If you have already had dust wipe results showing lead on the sills, windows are a natural place to start.

Replacement is not the same as demolition

Pulling out old windows with pry bars and saws can throw lead dust through the whole house. Lead-safe replacement uses containment, wet methods, HEPA vacuums and careful cleanup so the dust does not travel. Paid work that disturbs paint in a pre-1978 home falls under the EPA renovation, repair and painting rule, which requires certified firms and trained workers.

If the replacement is part of an official lead hazard fix, abatement rules apply instead. EPA explains how abatement and renovation differ, and the two kinds of certification are not interchangeable.

What replacement does and does not fix

A new window removes the lead paint on that window. It does not change the paint on the wall around it, the trim beside it or the porch outside, so the rest of the room still deserves a look. Finish with clearance testing so you know the dust is actually gone.

For houses with sound windows and other trouble spots, encapsulation or enclosure may cover the rest.

Common questions

Should I repaint my old windows instead?

Repainting a stable surface can be part of upkeep, but a window that rubs when it opens keeps making dust. An assessor can say whether repair, replacement or another method suits each window.

Does the contractor need special certification?

Yes. Paid work that disturbs paint in a pre-1978 home needs a lead-safe certified firm under the federal renovation rule, and Ohio requires licensed abatement contractors for lead hazard control work.

Will I need testing afterward?

Testing after the work is the way to confirm the dust is below the standard. See clearance testing and cleanup.
