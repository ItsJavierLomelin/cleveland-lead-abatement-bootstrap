Lead Risk Assessment for Cleveland Homes and Rentals

A risk assessment looks at the hazards, not just the paint: chipping surfaces, dust on floors and window sills, and bare soil near the house. In Cleveland it is also the starting point for rental owners applying for a lead safe certificate, and the step families take when a child has had a high blood lead test.

Inspection versus risk assessment

People mix these up constantly. An inspection says where lead paint is. A risk assessment says where it is hurting someone or likely to, and what should be done about it. According to EPA guidance on inspections and risk assessments, the assessment identifies hazards such as deteriorated paint, lead dust and contaminated soil, then recommends how to control them.

If you are not sure which one you need, the guide on how to tell if paint is lead paint starts from what you can see in the house.

What gets checked

The assessor looks at paint condition room by room, with extra attention to friction and impact surfaces: windows, doors, stairs, railings and porch parts. Dust wipe samples are taken from floors, window sills and window troughs and sent to a lab. Soil is checked where paint has dripped or flaked, usually around the foundation and play areas. For the details on the samples themselves, see lead dust and soil testing.

Since 2024, EPA treats any reportable level of lead in dust as a hazard, which is described on the EPA hazard standards page. That is one reason a lab-backed assessment is more useful than a quick visual check.

Why Cleveland rental owners start here

Cleveland City Council passed legislation in 2019 requiring rental owners to prove that their units are lead safe. The city FAQ says an owner of a pre-1978 rental obtains a lead risk assessment and can then apply for the lead safe certificate or an exemption. The full process is covered on the page about lead safe certificates for Cleveland rentals.

Owner-occupied homes do not need the certificate, but homeowners still use assessments before buying, before remodeling or after a child's lead test.

Common questions

Who can perform a lead risk assessment in Ohio?

Ohio requires a valid lead risk assessor license for a risk assessment. The state rule sets that out, and you can ask any provider for their license number.

Does an owner-occupied home need a Cleveland lead safe certificate?

No. The city says owner-occupied properties do not need to be certified, though a risk assessment can still be a sensible step.

What happens after the report?

The report lists hazards and recommended controls. Depending on what it finds, the work may be encapsulation, window replacement or full abatement, followed by clearance testing.
