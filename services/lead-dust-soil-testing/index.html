Lead Dust and Soil Testing Around Older Cleveland Homes

Lead paint becomes a health problem when it turns to dust, and that dust settles on floors, window sills and toys long before anyone notices a chip. Soil next to an old painted house can carry lead too, especially along the foundation where exterior paint has weathered for decades.

How lead dust testing works

A dust wipe test uses a pre-moistened wipe on a measured square of surface, such as a floor, a window sill or the trough where a window sash sits. The wipe goes to an accredited lab, and the lab reports how much lead was collected. EPA's dust-lead hazard standards now treat any reportable level as a hazard in pre-1978 homes, so a lab result is the number that matters.

Windows are the usual hot spot. When a painted sash slides against a painted frame, it grinds a little paint into fine dust each time, and that dust lands on the sill and the floor below.

Soil around the house

Exterior paint on siding, porches and trim sheds slowly with weather and repainting. Soil near the foundation, under porches and along drip lines can end up holding that paint. Soil sampling is worth considering where children play, where pets dig, or where you plan a vegetable bed.

This service covers lead from paint and dust. Drinking water is a separate question handled through your water supplier and plumbing, not through paint testing.

Where testing fits in the bigger picture

Dust and soil samples are usually part of a full risk assessment. They are also how a clearance exam confirms that a room is clean after work, which is covered under clearance testing and cleanup. If you only know that paint is peeling and want to understand what it might mean, start with the guide on telling whether paint is lead paint.

Common questions

Should I test the soil in my garden?

If the house is older than 1978 and has exterior paint that has flaked, it is reasonable to ask for soil sampling before planting food crops or setting up a play area near the foundation.

Can I clean lead dust up myself?

Ordinary sweeping and dry dusting can spread fine dust. EPA steps for making a home lead-safe cover safer cleaning habits, and a risk assessment tells you whether more is needed.

Is a dust test the same as a paint inspection?

No. An inspection checks paint. A dust test checks what has already come off the paint. The two answer different questions, which is why they are often combined.
