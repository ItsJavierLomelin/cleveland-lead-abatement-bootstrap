Lead Paint Testing and Inspection in Cleveland, OH

Most Cleveland housing was built long before lead paint was banned in 1978, and paint that looks sound can still contain lead. An inspection tests the painted surfaces in a home one by one, so you know where the lead is before a sale, a repaint or a rehab starts.

What a lead paint inspection tells you

A lead paint inspection answers one question: which painted or coated surfaces in this home contain lead-based paint. The inspector checks surfaces such as window sashes, door casings, trim, stair parts and porch components, usually with an instrument that reads the paint in place or with paint samples sent to a lab. The EPA explains the difference between inspections, risk assessments and abatements in plain language, and the short version is that an inspection finds lead paint but does not by itself say whether it is a hazard today.

That distinction matters in an old house. Intact lead paint on a wall that nobody touches is a different situation from the same paint on a window that slides open and shut every day. If you need to know about hazards, the next step is a lead risk assessment.

When people order testing

The usual triggers are buying an older home, preparing a rental, planning a remodel that will touch paint, or noticing paint that is chipping, chalking or peeling. Parents of young children often ask for it after a pediatrician mentions lead. Landlords ask because the City of Cleveland presumes that any rental built before January 1, 1978 has lead-based paint, as stated in the city ordinance.

A single swab on a single door only tells you about that door. A full inspection covers the whole home, which is why the results are useful for planning work and for deciding between encapsulation and removal.

Who is allowed to do it in Ohio

Ohio law does not let just anyone run these tests for hire. Under rule 3701-32-03 of the Ohio Administrative Code, a lead inspection requires a valid lead inspector or lead risk assessor license. When you call, ask who will do the testing and ask to see the license before you schedule anything.

If you are weighing testing against a bigger decision, the guide on testing, abatement or renovation walks through which service fits which situation.

Common questions

How do I know if my paint is lead paint?

You cannot tell by looking. Age is the first clue, since EPA reports that 87 percent of homes built before 1940 have some lead-based paint, and testing is the only way to confirm it on a specific surface.

Do I need lead testing if my house was built after 1978?

The City of Cleveland states that houses built after 1978 are exempt from its lead safe certificate law, so testing is mainly a question for homes built in 1978 or earlier.

What should I do with the results?

A positive result means the next call is about hazards and a plan, not panic. Keep painted surfaces in good condition, avoid dry scraping or sanding, and talk through a risk assessment and the right fix for each surface.
