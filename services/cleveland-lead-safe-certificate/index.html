Cleveland Lead Safe Certificate for Rental Property Owners

If you rent out a Cleveland property built before 1978, the city requires a lead safe certificate or an exemption. The process runs through the city portal, starts with a licensed lead risk assessment, and often turns up lead work to handle before the paperwork is finished.

Which properties are covered

The City of Cleveland's Lead Safe Program page says rental properties built before 1978 must obtain a Lead Safe Certification or an exemption, and that owner-occupied properties do not need to be certified. The requirement comes from legislation passed in 2019, and under the codified ordinance any rental built before January 1, 1978 is presumed to contain lead-based paint.

The city also notes that houses built after 1978 are exempt, so the year built is the first thing to confirm.

The steps in order

The city's step-by-step submission guide lists what you need: the rental registration number, the parcel number and year built, which you can look up on the Cuyahoga County property site, and email addresses for the owner and the risk assessor. The city FAQ adds that once you have a lead risk assessment you can apply for the certificate or a 20-year exemption through the city's Accela portal.

The risk assessment itself is described on the page about lead risk assessment. If the report finds hazards, plan for abatement, encapsulation or window replacement before you finalize.

Where lead work fits in

A certificate is paperwork, but the point of the program is safer housing. Rental units are where deteriorated paint tends to go unreported, because tenants are not the ones deciding on repairs. Fixing the hazard first, then confirming with clearance testing where required, is the sturdier path.

Rules and forms change. Check the city pages linked above for the current process before you submit.

Common questions

Does my owner-occupied house need a certificate?

No, the city says owner-occupied properties do not need to be certified. A risk assessment can still make sense if you are buying, renovating or have young children at home.

Who performs the lead risk assessment?

A licensed lead risk assessor. Ohio requires that license for risk assessments under rule 3701-32-03.

What is the 20-year exemption?

The city's FAQ says owners can apply for a certificate or a 20-year exemption after getting an assessment. The city's guide explains who qualifies, so read it before you apply.
