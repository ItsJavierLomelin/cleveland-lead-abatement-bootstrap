Lead Paint Removal and Abatement in Cleveland, OH

Abatement is the permanent fix for lead paint hazards: removing the paint, replacing the part that carries it, or sealing it in for good, under containment. In older Cleveland housing the usual targets are window sashes, door frames, porch parts and stair rails, where painted surfaces wear against each other.

What abatement means

Abatement is a defined term, not a synonym for repainting. HUD's abatement guidance describes it as removing the building component or the paint, or permanently enclosing lead-based paint hazards. The four recognized approaches are removal, replacement, enclosure and encapsulation, and a good assessor picks a different one for different surfaces in the same house.

Removal takes the paint off the surface. Replacement takes out the whole component, such as a window or door. Enclosure covers the paint with a rigid, durable barrier, and encapsulation seals it with a coating or bonded covering. Those last two are covered on the page about encapsulation and enclosure.

Why it is not a weekend project

EPA tells homeowners not to try to remove lead-based paint themselves. Dry scraping, sanding and heat guns turn paint into fine dust and fumes, and that is how a stable surface becomes a hazard. Abatement work is done with containment, protective gear, controlled cleanup and a final check.

Ohio also regulates who does the work. The Ohio Department of Health's abatement FAQ says a licensed lead abatement contractor or project designer is required in situations such as working on a property under a lead hazard control order, so ask about licensing up front.

After the work: clearance

Abatement is not finished when the crew leaves. A clearance examination checks that dust left behind is below the standard, and in Ohio that exam must be performed by a licensed lead inspector or risk assessor. You can read more on clearance testing and cleanup.

If your project is a remodel rather than a lead hazard fix, the rules are different. The page on renovating a pre-1978 Cleveland house explains the renovation rule and how it differs from abatement.

Common questions

Is lead abatement the same as lead remediation?

People use the words loosely, but abatement has a specific meaning: measures intended to permanently eliminate lead paint hazards. EPA draws the line between abatement and renovation work based on the reason for the work.

Do I have to remove all the lead paint in my house?

Not necessarily. A risk assessment points to the hazards, and some surfaces can be handled by enclosure or encapsulation instead of removal.

Can I scrape lead paint myself to save a step?

EPA advises against it. Disturbing the paint is what creates the dust, so the work is better left to trained, licensed people.
